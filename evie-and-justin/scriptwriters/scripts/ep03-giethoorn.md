## Logline
Two kittens look for the streets of a village that doesn't have any, and discover that the water was dug on purpose.

## Script

**COLD OPEN (0:30)**
[Black screen. Soft splash. A paw tag glows.]
JUSTIN (whisper): Evie. Where is the road?
EVIE: There isn't one.
JUSTIN: Then where do the *cars* go?
EVIE: Look!
[Smash cut: a quiet canal, thatched houses, flower boxes, a tiny arched bridge. Cats standing on the little bridge.]
EVIE (to camera): Hello, friends! Tonight's bedtime adventure is a village with water for streets.
JUSTIN: Welcome to Evie and Justin's Little Adventures!
[TITLE CARD]

**ARRIVAL & THE QUESTION (1:00)**
[Wide: a canal village in golden light. Small islands with houses, bridges everywhere.]
EVIE: Ooh, the whole village is made of tiny islands.
JUSTIN: With bridges. Lots of bridges. More than a hundred and seventy, I read.
EVIE: And no cars in the old part of the village.
JUSTIN: But who builds a village like this? Did somebody just look at a map and say, "Let's put water here"?
[Paw tags glow. Question card: "Who dug the water?"]
EVIE: That's tonight's mystery.
[A tiny *ting* from somewhere across the reeds. Evie turns. Justin is busy with a duck.]

**EXPLORE & DISCOVER (5:00)**
*Scene 1 — Whisper boat (1:15)*
[They climb into a small electric boat. Life jackets clearly on. Soft hum.]
JUSTIN: Why is it so quiet?
EVIE: It's called a whisper boat. It runs on electricity, so it doesn't make a lot of noise.
JUSTIN: Good. I don't want to wake up the ducks.
EVIE: Or the people. People live in these houses.
JUSTIN: Right. So we're quiet guests. Paws in the boat, voices down.
EVIE: Wave hello. Don't peek in windows.
JUSTIN: I wasn't going to peek. I was going to… look *politely*.
[A gentle laugh. They glide under a bridge.]

*Scene 2 — The mystery of the goat horns (1:15)*
[A bench. A storybook illustration appears: people with spades in a marsh.]
EVIE: Justin, there's a story about how this village got its name.
JUSTIN: Go on.
EVIE: A long time ago, people came here and dug up something in the ground. Goat horns.
JUSTIN: Goat… horns?
EVIE: The story says the horns came from goats that were lost in a great flood in the year 1170. And so the settlers called the place *Geytenhoren*.
JUSTIN: And now it's Giethoorn?
EVIE: That's right. Goat horns.
JUSTIN: So the village is named after a flood, a goat, and somebody's very strange discovery.
EVIE: It's a story, Justin. It might be true, it might be a legend. But it's a fun one.

*Scene 3 — What is peat? (1:30)*
[They stop at a small display or at the water's edge. Close-up on dark, soft soil.]
JUSTIN: Okay, but why dig anything at all? Why not just build on dry ground?
EVIE: Because the ground here was *peat*. Peat is old plants that never fully rotted, squashed together for thousands of years.
JUSTIN: Squashed plants. Delicious.
EVIE: And people could dry it and burn it for warmth.
JUSTIN: So peat is like… firewood from the ground.
EVIE: Yes! So people began to dig it out, by hand. In the thirteenth century.
[Storybook flashback: villagers with spades; dark blocks drying in rows; flat boats carrying peat.]
JUSTIN: And digging peat leaves holes.
EVIE: Big holes. And water fills holes.
JUSTIN: So the lakes…
EVIE: …came from digging. And the people dug little ditches to carry the peat in boats. Those ditches became the canals.
JUSTIN: Wait. The canals are the *streets* because they were the *roads* for peat?
EVIE: Exactly. Water was the road.

*Scene 4 — Living with water (1:00)*
[They float past houses, boats tied up, a bike on a bridge.]
EVIE: Now look at how people live here today.
JUSTIN: A boat in front of every house. Like a parking space, but wetter.
EVIE: Bridges to cross. Gardens on tiny islands.
JUSTIN: So the water isn't a problem here. It's… the neighbourhood.
EVIE: But people still take care. If you take the peat out and water comes in, you have to look after it.
JUSTIN: Look after it how?
EVIE: Keeping the water clean, keeping the banks in good shape, caring for the nature around the village.
JUSTIN: Same as last time. Live *with* water. Don't fight it. Take care of it.

**CONCEPT EXPLAINED (2:00)**
[Storybook cutaway: a slice of the ground. Layers: grass, dark peat, water.]
EVIE: Justin, here's the secret of this place. Peat was a gift from the ground, but taking it changed the land.
JUSTIN: Gifts have consequences. Got it.
[Cards, one at a time, paw-stamped.]
EVIE: One — **Dig it.** People dug peat for fuel.
JUSTIN: Two — **Water fills the gaps.** Holes became lakes and ditches became canals.
EVIE: Three — **Live with it.** The village grew on little islands, with boats and bridges.
JUSTIN: So a village was made by digging a *very* big hole. Slowly. For a long, long time.
EVIE: And then people decided to stay, and make it home.
JUSTIN: That's pretty clever for people with spades.
EVIE: Water can be a road, a home and a neighbour.
[Gentle music swell.]

**RECAP + TEASER (0:45)**
JUSTIN: Recap! Who dug the water?
EVIE: People did! They dug peat…
JUSTIN: …and the holes filled with water…
EVIE: …and the village learned to live with it.
JUSTIN: Next, I want to go back *way* further. Past roads, past boats, past peat.
EVIE: Ooh. How far?
JUSTIN: Five thousand years. To some very big stones.
[Teaser card: "Next: The Sleeping Giants".]

**GOODNIGHT OUTRO (0:30)**
[Dusk on the canal. Lanterns come on in the windows. Music slows.]
EVIE (yawning): It's so quiet. I can hear the water whispering.
JUSTIN: Even the boats whisper here.
EVIE: Goodnight, Giethoorn.
JUSTIN: Goodnight, ducks. Goodnight, bridges.
[Paw tags glow, then dim. A last, faint *ting…* across the reeds. Fade out.]

## Shot notes / visual ideas
- Everything at kitten level: water line, reeds, bridge underbellies. Warm light on thatch.
- Life jackets visible on the cats whenever they are in the boat; keep their movements calm.
- Quiet-guest rule: no peeking into windows, no rushing, no loud noises. Show respect for residents and nature.
- Flashbacks flat storybook style; peat in dark brown, water in soft blue.
- The bell: the faintest "ting" again, off-screen. Evie notices; Justin gets distracted (a duck). Do not explain.
- Low-compute friendly: stills + parallax + mouth swap.
