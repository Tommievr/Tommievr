## Logline
Four friends walk toward a barrier with giant gates, and learn how the Dutch protect the land without shutting the sea out forever.

## Script

**COLD OPEN (0:30)**
[Black screen. Deep waves. *Ting.* Paw tags glow.]
TOFFEE: Is that a wall?
COTTON: A very long one.
TOFFEE: The longest wall in the whole wide world?
JUSTIN: Let's not get dramatic. That's my job.
[Smash cut: a vast line of concrete pillars across blue-grey water, gulls above. Four small figures at the shoreline.]
EVIE (to camera): Hello, friends! We brought two new friends to the sea!
COTTON: Hello. [waves shyly, bell rings]
TOFFEE: Hi! Hi! Hi!
JUSTIN: Welcome to Evie and Justin's Little Adventures!
[TITLE CARD]

**ARRIVAL & THE QUESTION (1:00)**
[Wide: the Oosterscheldekering stretching across the bay. Island with visitor buildings.]
COTTON: It's so big.
TOFFEE: It's like a giant comb for the sea!
JUSTIN: Not bad, Toffee. Not bad at all.
JUSTIN: This is the Oosterscheldekering. It's about nine kilometres long.
TOFFEE: Nine kilometres? That's a million hops!
EVIE: Ooh, look! Tall concrete pillars, with huge steel gates in between.
JUSTIN: But last time, the Afsluitdijk closed the sea *completely*. Why does this one have gaps?
COTTON: Is the sea… getting in?
JUSTIN: Suspicious gaps. I like it.
[Paw tags glow. Question card: "Why leave gaps in a wall against the sea?"]
EVIE: A puzzle! Let's find out.
COTTON: Together?
EVIE: Always together.
TOFFEE: Even if there's mud?
JUSTIN: Especially if there's mud.

**EXPLORE & DISCOVER (5:00)**
*Scene 1 — The storm that changed everything (1:15)*
[A quiet museum corner, with a storybook illustration of a stormy night.]
EVIE: A long time ago, in 1953, there was a very big storm.
COTTON: What happened?
EVIE: The sea rose very high and broke through dikes. Water rushed over the land in the night.
[Soft storybook flashback: dark waves, lit windows, rooftops. Calm music.]
EVIE: Many families had to leave their homes. It was a very hard time.
TOFFEE (very quiet): That sounds so sad.
COTTON: It's okay to feel that. [bell rings softly]
EVIE: But people helped each other. They rebuilt their homes, together.
JUSTIN: And that's when the Dutch said, never again.
EVIE: Together, they decided to make the land safer.
COTTON: So they built big walls?
EVIE: Yes. A huge plan, called the Delta Works. Dams, barriers and gates along the coast of Zeeland.
JUSTIN: A *very* big plan.

*Scene 2 — The big idea (1:15)*
[They walk across the island toward a lookout.]
JUSTIN: Okay. But if you want to stop the sea, why not just close it completely?
JUSTIN: One big solid wall. Simple. Done.
EVIE: That was the first plan. A solid dam.
COTTON: What changed?
EVIE: People spoke up. They asked: what about nature?
JUSTIN: And the fishing?
EVIE: Yes! The Oosterschelde is salty water, with oysters, mussels, seals and birds.
TOFFEE: Sea creatures need the sea?
JUSTIN: Toffee, that's the smartest thing you've said all day.
TOFFEE: Thank you! What did I say?
EVIE: Imagine a house with every window sealed shut. It would feel very stuffy.
COTTON: Poor house.
TOFFEE: Poor oysters!
JUSTIN: So a solid wall would keep the sea out, but it would also keep the sea *away*.
COTTON: And that wouldn't be fair to the birds.
EVIE: The sea likes to move in and out, too. So the plan changed.
EVIE: The barrier was built with gates that stay *open*.
JUSTIN: So the sea can come and go, like normal.
EVIE: And the gates close only when a storm pushes the sea dangerously high.
COTTON: Oh! That's kind. For the sea, and for us.

*Scene 3 — The pillars and the gates (1:15)*
[Cut-away: simple animation of pillars and gates. A cartoon sliding gate in the middle.]
EVIE: There are sixty-five pillars, with sixty-two big steel gates between them.
TOFFEE: One, two, three, four, five…
COTTON: Toffee, don't count them all.
TOFFEE: …seventeen, eighteen… oh no. I lost count.
JUSTIN: Sixty-five. Evie just told you.
EVIE: Think of the pillars as doorframes. And the gates are the doors.
JUSTIN: Doorframes that stand in the sea all day. Respect.
COTTON: They look very patient.
JUSTIN: So: doorframes, doors, and one very big sea outside.
TOFFEE: Can I try one?
COTTON: Toffee, no.
TOFFEE: Just a small one?
JUSTIN: Absolutely not. Look, they're controlled from a special control centre, on the island.
EVIE: People there watch the weather, the tide and the waves.
JUSTIN: Like lifeguards, but for a whole sea.
EVIE: When a storm is coming, they close the gates.
JUSTIN: And the sea waits outside.
COTTON: How many times have they closed?
EVIE: Only about thirty times since the barrier opened in 1986.
TOFFEE: Thirty? What do the gates do the rest of the time?
JUSTIN: They wait.
TOFFEE: That sounds boring.
COTTON: Waiting is the job, Toffee. A good guard is ready, not busy.
TOFFEE: So it's mostly… a very long gate, watching.
JUSTIN: A quiet guard.
COTTON: I like quiet guards.
EVIE: Me too. They notice everything and never brag about it.
TOFFEE: I brag about everything.
JUSTIN: We noticed.

*Scene 4 — Toffee's wobble (0:30)*
[They stand at a safe lookout behind a railing. Toffee leans over to peek at the water.]
[Evie's tag turns RED. Low hum. X pulse.]
EVIE: Red! Low hum, X sign. Pause!
TOFFEE: Look at the waves, they're so—
COTTON: Toffee, back from the edge!
[Toffee wobbles, Cotton pulls him back with her wool. Her bell rings.]
TOFFEE: Phew. Thanks.
COTTON: Stay behind the railing. The sea is stronger than it looks.
[Evie's tag turns GREEN. Soft chime. Check-mark pulse.]
EVIE: Green. Behind the railing is safe.
TOFFEE: I only wanted to say hello to it.
JUSTIN: You can wave from here.
TOFFEE: [waving] Hello, sea!
JUSTIN: Safety first, even for gates.

*Scene 5 — Evie's what-if (0:45)*
[Behind the railing. Evie closes her eyes. A soft dreamy storybook cut-away: the sea as a big, friendly dog.]
EVIE: What if the sea is like a big, friendly dog?
JUSTIN: A dog? Okay. I'm listening.
EVIE: Most days it wanders around and plays. That's the open gates.
TOFFEE: And in a storm?
EVIE: Then the dog gets the zoomies!
JUSTIN: Zoomies. Of course.
EVIE: Big wavy zoomies. You wouldn't want to stand in the way.
TOFFEE: I would stand behind the railing!
COTTON: That's the best place to stand.
COTTON: So the gate is like a baby gate in a doorway?
EVIE: Yes! You open it so the dog can wander, and you close it when the zoomies start.
JUSTIN: And when the zoomies stop?
EVIE: You open the gate again. Everybody's happy.
TOFFEE: I would like a baby gate for my zoomies.
COTTON: We all would.
[Paw tags glow softly.]

**CONCEPT EXPLAINED (2:00)**
[Storybook cutaway: a barrier from the side. Normal day: gates up, water flowing. Storm: gates down.]
EVIE: Let's explain it simply. A storm surge barrier is a door for the sea.
JUSTIN: A storm surge is when a storm pushes the sea up higher than normal.
TOFFEE: Like a giant puff of breath?
JUSTIN: Close enough, Toffee. Close enough.
EVIE: Imagine blowing on a bowl of soup. The soup slides to one side.
COTTON: And the soup is the sea?
EVIE: Yes. A storm pushes the sea toward the land, and the sea piles up high.
TOFFEE: Soup, but scary.
JUSTIN: Soup that needs a gate.
[Cards appear, one at a time, paw-stamped.]
JUSTIN: One: **Normal day: open.** Tides come and go, nature stays healthy.
COTTON: Two: **Storm coming: close.** People lower the gates before the water rises too high.
TOFFEE: Three: **Storm over: open again.** The sea gets its space back.
EVIE: So the barrier doesn't fight the sea every day. It only steps in when it must.
JUSTIN: That's engineering with manners.
EVIE: And with respect, for both people and nature.
JUSTIN: Quiz time! What if the gates stayed shut forever?
COTTON: The oysters and the seals would miss the sea.
JUSTIN: And what if the gates stayed open in a storm?
TOFFEE: Then the sea would come in and… that's not good.
EVIE: Right. So the guards must choose the right moment.
JUSTIN: Open when it's calm. Closed when it's not.
EVIE: Gentle with the sea, and gentle with the land.
TOFFEE: And no touching the gates.
JUSTIN: Finally, he learns.
TOFFEE: I only learned the touching part.
COTTON: Like a good guard. Quiet, careful, and kind.
TOFFEE: A guard with very big doors.
[Gentle music swell.]

**RECAP + TEASER (0:45)**
JUSTIN: Recap! Why leave gaps in a wall against the sea?
EVIE: So the sea and its nature can live normally…
COTTON: …and the gates close only when a storm is coming.
TOFFEE: And a quiet guard watches the whole time!
JUSTIN: Next time, we go where the sea comes and goes… and we walk on the bottom.
TOFFEE: The *bottom?*
EVIE: Mudflats!
TOFFEE: Muddy hooves! Yay!
COTTON: Oh dear. Evie, please bring towels.
JUSTIN: And a very big bath.
[Teaser card: "Next: Walking on the Sea Floor".]

**GOODNIGHT OUTRO (0:30)**
[Dusk. Lights on the barrier turn on one by one. Four small shapes sit on a quiet shore. Music slows.]
COTTON: It's strong, but it feels gentle.
EVIE: Like a very big blanket.
TOFFEE (yawning): With big doors.
TOFFEE: My birthday is soon. Can the party be here?
COTTON: I'd like to stay little at the party. Just a bit longer.
EVIE: (smiling) We'll see.
JUSTIN: Goodnight, sea. Thanks for waiting outside.
EVIE: Goodnight, oysters and seals and birds.
COTTON: Goodnight, gates. Thank you for watching over us.
TOFFEE: Goodnight, long wall. I mean, gentle guard.
[Paw tags glow, then dim. Bell *ting…* Fade out.]

## Shot notes / visual ideas
- Scale: pillars as rhythmic verticals marching to the horizon; the four small characters tiny at the shoreline.
- 1953 flashback: gentle, storybook, no frightening imagery — dark blues, lit windows, helpers. Calm music. No bodies or distress shots.
- Gates animation: simple sliding doors; "open" and "closed" in two panels. Soft music, calm, no dramatic crashing.
- Four-voice scenes: one short line per speaker; Cotton gentle, Toffee excited, Justin dry, Evie soft.
- Safety: all four stay behind railings and on visitor paths; Toffee's wobble is corrected immediately.
- Low-compute friendly: stills + parallax + mouth swap.
- Tag signal (GREEN = chime + check-mark, RED = low hum + X): red at the water's edge, green behind the railing. Birthday/stay-little foreshadow in the outro (light, unresolved). No casualty numbers anywhere in this script.
