## Logline
Four friends walk toward a barrier with giant gates, and learn how the Dutch protect the land without shutting the sea out forever.

## Script

**COLD OPEN (0:30)**
[Black screen. Deep waves. *Ting.* Paw tags glow.]
TOFFEE: Is that a wall?
COTTON: A very long one.
[Smash cut: a vast line of concrete pillars across blue-grey water, gulls above. Four small figures at the shoreline.]
EVIE (to camera): Hello, friends! We brought two new friends to the sea!
JUSTIN: Welcome to Evie and Justin's Little Adventures!
[TITLE CARD]

**ARRIVAL & THE QUESTION (1:00)**
[Wide: the Oosterscheldekering stretching across the bay. Island with visitor buildings.]
COTTON: It's so big.
TOFFEE: It's like a giant comb for the sea!
JUSTIN: This is the Oosterscheldekering. It's about nine kilometres long.
EVIE: And it's made of tall concrete pillars with huge steel gates in between.
JUSTIN: But last time, the Afsluitdijk closed the sea *completely*. Why does this one have gaps?
COTTON: Is the sea… getting in?
[Paw tags glow. Question card: "Why leave gaps in a wall against the sea?"]
EVIE: Let's find out.

**EXPLORE & DISCOVER (5:00)**
*Scene 1 — The storm that changed everything (1:30)*
[A quiet museum corner, with a storybook illustration of a stormy night.]
EVIE: A long time ago, in 1953, there was a terrible storm.
COTTON: What happened?
EVIE: The sea rose very high and broke through dikes. Water rushed over the land in the night.
[Soft storybook flashback: dark waves, lit windows, rooftops, helicopters, boats of rescuers.]
EVIE: More than eighteen hundred people died. Many families lost their homes.
TOFFEE: (very quiet) That's so sad.
JUSTIN: And that's when the Dutch said, never again.
COTTON: So they built big walls?
EVIE: Yes. A huge plan, called the Delta Works. Dams, barriers and gates along the coast of Zeeland.
JUSTIN: A *very* big plan.

*Scene 2 — The big idea (1:15)*
[They walk across the island toward a lookout.]
JUSTIN: Okay. But if you want to stop the sea, why not just close it completely?
EVIE: That was the first plan. A solid dam.
COTTON: What changed?
EVIE: People asked: what about the nature? The Oosterschelde is salty water with oysters, mussels, seals and birds.
TOFFEE: Sea creatures need the sea?
EVIE: Yes! So the plan changed. The barrier was built with gates that stay *open*.
JUSTIN: So the sea can come and go, like normal.
EVIE: And the gates close only when a storm pushes the sea dangerously high.
COTTON: Oh! That's kind. For the sea, and for us.

*Scene 3 — The pillars and the gates (1:15)*
[Cut-away: simple animation of pillars and gates. A cartoon sliding gate in the middle.]
EVIE: There are sixty-five pillars, with sixty-two big steel gates between them.
JUSTIN: That's a lot of doors.
TOFFEE: Can I try one?
COTTON: Toffee, no.
TOFFEE: Just a small one?
JUSTIN: Absolutely not. Look, they're controlled from a special control centre. People watch the weather, the tide and the waves.
EVIE: When a storm is coming, they close the gates.
JUSTIN: And the sea waits outside.
COTTON: How many times have they closed?
EVIE: Only about thirty times since the barrier opened in 1986.
TOFFEE: So it's mostly… a very long gate, watching.
JUSTIN: A quiet guard.

*Scene 4 — Toffee's wobble (0:30)*
[They stand at a safe lookout behind a railing. Toffee leans over to peek at the water.]
TOFFEE: Look at the waves, they're so—
COTTON: Toffee, back from the edge!
[Toffee wobbles, Cotton pulls him back with her wool. Her bell rings.]
TOFFEE: Phew. Thanks.
COTTON: Stay behind the railing. The sea is stronger than it looks.
JUSTIN: Safety first, even for gates.

**CONCEPT EXPLAINED (2:00)**
[Storybook cutaway: a barrier from the side. Normal day: gates up, water flowing. Storm: gates down.]
EVIE: Let's explain it simply. A storm surge barrier is a door for the sea.
[Cards appear, one at a time, paw-stamped.]
JUSTIN: One — **Normal day: open.** Tides come and go, nature stays healthy.
COTTON: Two — **Storm coming: close.** People lower the gates before the water rises too high.
TOFFEE: Three — **Storm over: open again.** The sea gets its space back.
EVIE: So the barrier doesn't fight the sea every day. It only steps in when it must.
JUSTIN: That's engineering with manners.
EVIE: And with respect, for both people and nature.
COTTON: Like a good guard. Quiet, careful, and kind.
TOFFEE: A guard with very big doors.
[Gentle music swell.]

**RECAP + TEASER (0:45)**
JUSTIN: Recap! Why leave gaps in a wall against the sea?
EVIE: So the sea and its nature can live normally…
COTTON: …and the gates close only when a storm is coming.
TOFFEE: And a quiet guard watches the whole time!
JUSTIN: Next time, we go where the sea comes and goes… and we walk on the bottom.
TOFFEE: The *bottom?*
EVIE: Mudflats!
[Teaser card: "Next: Walking on the Sea Floor".]

**GOODNIGHT OUTRO (0:30)**
[Dusk. Lights on the barrier turn on one by one. Four small shapes sit on a quiet shore. Music slows.]
COTTON: It's strong, but it feels gentle.
EVIE: Like a very big blanket.
TOFFEE (yawning): With big doors.
JUSTIN: Goodnight, sea. Thanks for waiting outside.
COTTON: Goodnight, gates.
[Paw tags glow, then dim. Bell *ting…* Fade out.]

## Shot notes / visual ideas
- Scale: pillars as rhythmic verticals marching to the horizon; the four small characters tiny at the shoreline.
- 1953 flashback: gentle, storybook, no frightening imagery — dark blues, lit windows, helpers. Calm music. No bodies or distress shots.
- Gates animation: simple sliding doors; "open" and "closed" in two panels. Soft music, calm, no dramatic crashing.
- Four-voice scenes: one short line per speaker; Cotton gentle, Toffee excited, Justin dry, Evie soft.
- Safety: all four stay behind railings and on visitor paths; Toffee's wobble is corrected immediately.
- Low-compute friendly: stills + parallax + mouth swap.
