## Logline
Two kittens find an island with no sea around it, and learn how the Dutch turned seabed into farmland.

## Script

**COLD OPEN (0:30)**
[Black screen. Seagull cries… but no water sound. Just wind in grass.]
JUSTIN (whisper): Evie. There are gulls. Where's the sea?
EVIE: (gasp) Justin, look at the signs. They say… *island.*
[Smash cut: a long, low green ridge rising out of a flat sea of fields. Cats on top. Tractor far away.]
JUSTIN: An island. In a field.
JUSTIN: I packed my swimming goggles for nothing.
EVIE (to camera): Hello, friends! Tonight we found an island with no sea!
JUSTIN: Welcome to Evie and Justin's Little Adventures!
[TITLE CARD]

**ARRIVAL & THE QUESTION (1:00)**
[Wide: the ridge, a church and a few old buildings on a small hill. Fields to every horizon.]
EVIE: This place is called Schokland.
JUSTIN: Last time we saw a sea turn into a lake. This is worse. This is a sea turning into… carrots.
EVIE: Maybe potatoes.
JUSTIN: Evie. People used to live on this island. Boats came and went. So where did the water go?
EVIE: I keep imagining it, Justin. Grey waves right where that tractor is. Little boats bobbing past our paws.
JUSTIN: And I keep imagining a very confused fish, standing in a field, checking its watch.
EVIE: Fish don't wear watches.
JUSTIN: This one would. It's very late for a fish.
[Paw tags glow. Question card: "Where did the sea go?"]
EVIE: Let's find out.
[Very faint: *ting…* a tiny bell, far away. Evie's ear twitches. Justin doesn't notice.]

**EXPLORE & DISCOVER (5:00)**
*Scene 1 — The island and its hills (1:15)*
[They walk the ridge. A small mound with houses, a church.]
EVIE: Look how the ground rises here, like a gentle hill.
JUSTIN: A hill in a country that's flat. Suspicious.
EVIE: People built these little mounds themselves. They're called *terpen*, so houses stayed dry when the water rose.
JUSTIN: Wait. They built their own hills?
EVIE: With their paws. Well, with their hands.
JUSTIN: How do you even build a hill? Do you just… pile up a lot of ground and hope?
EVIE: Basically! Like a sandcastle, but for a whole house. Raise the ground, and the water has to stay lower.
JUSTIN: A sandcastle that people live in. I want one. With a snack room.
EVIE: Imagine waking up on a little hill, with water all around, and your whole village right there with you.
JUSTIN: Cosy. Splashy. Slightly damp. Mostly cosy.
[Storybook flashback: small houses on mounds, grey water all around, boats tied up.]

*Scene 2 — The stormy years (1:15)*
[Cosy museum corner: a picture of the island, getting smaller.]
EVIE: The island was a long strip of peat in a stormy sea. Piece by piece, the water nibbled it away.
JUSTIN: Nibbled?
EVIE: Like a mouse with a very big cracker. Nibble, nibble, nibble.
JUSTIN: Rude. Mice should ask first.
EVIE: A big flood in 1825 did terrible damage.
JUSTIN: And that's when everyone left?
EVIE: Not right away. In 1859 the government decided people could not keep living here safely. About six hundred and fifty people moved away.
JUSTIN: Six hundred and fifty. That's a lot of goodbyes.
EVIE: I think it was a sad day. They loved their little island.
JUSTIN: They took their *houses* with them?
EVIE: They took the houses apart and used the wood and bricks to build new homes.
JUSTIN: That's the biggest packing job ever. Where do you put the boxes? There's no *box* big enough for a roof.
EVIE: So they carried it piece by piece. A window frame here, a plank there.
JUSTIN: And nobody said "are we there yet"?
EVIE: Probably someone did. Probably several someones.
[Flashback: families carrying planks; a little boat loaded with a window frame.]

*Scene 3 — Footprints (0:45)*
[A glass case. Soft light.]
EVIE: Justin, look. Footprints. Really old ones.
JUSTIN: Whose?
EVIE: People from about four thousand years ago, found here in 1984.
[Justin places his paw next to a drawing of the print, looks up, quiet for a moment.]
JUSTIN: So people have been walking here since… forever.
EVIE: Since before the sea came, and long after.
JUSTIN: Somebody stood right here, Evie. Maybe they looked up at the sky, just like us.
EVIE: And wondered what was for dinner.
JUSTIN: Obviously. That never changes.

*Scene 4 — The sea goes away (1:45)*
[They climb to a lookout over the fields. Fields stretch like a green-gold quilt.]
JUSTIN: Okay. Big question. You told me this was a sea. Where is it?
EVIE: Not gone. *Moved.* Long after the people left, the Dutch built a ring dike around this part of the sea.
JUSTIN: Same as Afsluitdijk. A wall.
EVIE: Yes! A big wall in a circle, like a hug around the water.
JUSTIN: A hug that keeps the sea out. And then what? You can't just ask the water to leave. I've tried that with the bath.
EVIE: And then they pumped the water out, over the dike, until the sea floor was dry. Dry land, with rich soil.
JUSTIN: They pumped out a *sea*?
EVIE: Pumps. Windmills first, long ago. Big machines later.
JUSTIN: How does a pump even work? Does it slurp?
EVIE: A bit like a giant straw, Justin. It lifts the water up and sends it somewhere else.
JUSTIN: Slurp, slurp, over the wall. Got it. Best straw ever.
EVIE: Day after day, until the mud began to dry.
JUSTIN: And in 1942…
EVIE: …the area around Schokland was dry. The island stopped being an island. We call that area the Noordoostpolder.
JUSTIN: Polder. That's the word for land that used to be sea?
EVIE: Land that used to be under water. Then the water was taken away.
JUSTIN: Wait. So the farmers are driving where the fish used to swim.
EVIE: Imagine, Justin. Where a boat once sailed, now a tractor goes by, and the grass is tall and golden.
[Flashback sequence: dike closing a circle, pumps working, mud turning to grass, a tractor driving where a boat once sailed.]
JUSTIN: So the sea floor turned into farms. And where we're standing…
EVIE: …was an island. And now it's a hill in the middle of a field.
[Faint *ting…* again, this time a bit clearer. Evie looks at Justin. Justin looks at his own paws.]
JUSTIN: Wasn't me.
EVIE: I didn't say it was.
JUSTIN: You looked at me.
EVIE: I look at you a lot. You're nice to look at.
JUSTIN: Hmm. Fair.

**CONCEPT EXPLAINED (2:00)**
[Storybook cutaway: a bathtub with a little island, shown from the side.]
EVIE: Here's the idea, Justin. Imagine a bathtub with a toy island in it.
JUSTIN: Okay. A very serious bathtub.
EVIE: And you want a dry patch around the toy island, so you can play there.
JUSTIN: Dry patch in a bathtub. Ambitious. Go on.
EVIE: First you put up a wall around the part you want dry.
JUSTIN: Step one — **Wall it off.**
EVIE: Then you pump the water out of that part and over the wall.
JUSTIN: Step two — **Pump it out.**
EVIE: Then the ground dries, plants grow, and people can live and farm there.
JUSTIN: Step three — **Make it land.**
[Three paw-stamp cards, one at a time.]
EVIE: That's **land reclamation**. Taking back land from the water, carefully.
JUSTIN: Why "carefully"? Why not quickly?
EVIE: Because the wall has to be strong, and the pumping takes a long time. Dry land is a big, patient project.
JUSTIN: Like waiting for a cake. But the cake is a whole farm.
EVIE: A very big cake, yes.
JUSTIN: But then the water wants to come back.
EVIE: That's why the pumps never really stop. And people keep checking the dikes.
JUSTIN: So it's like the bathtub wall needs a grown-up checking it every day.
EVIE: Exactly. A whole country of grown-ups, checking the walls.
JUSTIN: Same as the Afsluitdijk. The sea doesn't give up. We just keep a good eye on it.
EVIE: And that's why a place like Schokland matters. It stands high, so we remember what the land used to be.
JUSTIN: A hill that says "hello, I used to be an island."
EVIE: And I love that it still says it.
[Gentle music swell.]

**RECAP + TEASER (0:45)**
JUSTIN: Recap time. Where did the sea go?
EVIE: It didn't disappear. People built a wall and pumped it out…
JUSTIN: …so the sea floor became land. And the island became a hill in the field.
EVIE: Three steps. Wall it off, pump it out, make it land.
JUSTIN: And then keep checking the wall, forever. Easy. Hardly any work at all.
EVIE: Next time, can we visit a village where the water is *still there*?
JUSTIN: Water instead of roads?
EVIE: Water instead of roads!
JUSTIN: Finally, a place where my goggles come in handy.
[Teaser card: "Next: The Village With No Roads".]

**GOODNIGHT OUTRO (0:30)**
[Sunset over the fields. Music slows. The kittens sit on the little hill.]
EVIE (yawning): Imagine the ships, long ago, sailing right here.
JUSTIN: Imagine the tractors, long from now, still here.
EVIE: And imagine all the people who walked here before us.
JUSTIN: And all the fish who are very, very confused.
EVIE: Goodnight, Schokland.
JUSTIN: Goodnight, little island. Thanks for staying tall.
[Paw tags glow, then dim. Very faint *ting…* far away. Fade out.]

- Flashbacks flat storybook style; the "sea" version in blue-grey, the "farmland" version in warm golden greens.
- The bell: a tiny, soft "ting" mixed very low. Never explain it. Evie notices, Justin doesn't (yet).
- Bathtub diagram: simple, cute, cats as markers; no text-heavy slides.
- Respect: stay on paths; do not show cats climbing on or sitting on the museum or the old church; keep to the walkways.
- Low-compute friendly: stills + parallax + mouth swap.
