## Logline
Two kittens find giant stones that are five thousand years old, and learn how people with no machines could have moved them. Then a bell rings in the heath.

## Script

**COLD OPEN (0:30)**
[Black screen. Deep, slow drumbeat like a heartbeat. A paw tag glows.]
JUSTIN (whisper): Evie. Those rocks are… *huge.*
EVIE: And they're lying in a row like they're asleep.
JUSTIN (whisper): Should we tiptoe? I feel like we should tiptoe.
EVIE (whisper): Tiptoe. Whisper. Maybe even hold our breath.
JUSTIN: I can't hold my breath and talk. I tried once. It was a squeak.
[Smash cut: a long line of enormous grey stones under old oak trees, with a path alongside. Cats tiny beside them.]
EVIE (to camera): Hello, friends! Tonight's adventure goes back five thousand years.
JUSTIN: Welcome to Evie and Justin's Little Adventures!
[TITLE CARD]

**ARRIVAL & THE QUESTION (1:00)**
[Wide: the long stone monument in the woods, a signposted path, a visitor centre in the distance.]
EVIE: These are called hunebedden. One of them is a *hunebed*.
JUSTIN: Hunebed. Say it with me. HOO-nuh-bet.
EVIE: HOO-nuh-bet. There are more than fifty of them in Drenthe, this part of the Netherlands.
JUSTIN: Fifty! That's a lot of sleeping giants. Somebody must be very tired.
EVIE: And look how they're made. Big stones standing up, and even bigger stones lying across the top, like a roof.
JUSTIN: They're built from stones bigger than a bicycle shed.
EVIE: Bigger than that.
JUSTIN: Evie, if I find a stone that big, I'm going to need a very long nap just to think about it.
EVIE: I just keep wondering what it felt like to be here, back when these stones were brand new.
JUSTIN: Brand new stones. Still shiny, probably.
EVIE: Probably not shiny, Justin.
JUSTIN: So here's my question. People built this without trucks, cranes or even *wheels* on big carts. How did they move these stones?
[Paw tags glow. Question card: "How do you move a giant stone?"]
EVIE: Let's find out.

**EXPLORE & DISCOVER (5:00)**
*Scene 1 — Where do the stones come from? (1:15)*
[They stand beside a huge grey boulder. Small, careful steps. Not climbing.]
JUSTIN: Okay, the stones are here. But Drenthe is sandy. There are no mountains. Where did they come from?
EVIE: From far away. Up north, in Scandinavia.
JUSTIN: Scandinavia? Did somebody mail them?
EVIE: Ice mailed them! A very long time ago, huge glaciers pushed over the land and carried boulders south.
[Storybook flashback: a vast blue-white glacier sliding slowly across the land, dropping rocks as it melts.]
JUSTIN: Wait, how does ice carry a rock? Ice is just frozen water. I can't even carry my dinner bowl.
EVIE: Think of a giant, slow, frozen river. It doesn't carry the rocks in its paws. It just keeps sliding and scoops them up along the way.
JUSTIN: Like when I walk through the garden and burrs stick to my tail.
EVIE: Exactly like that. Only much, much bigger. And much, much colder.
JUSTIN: So when the ice melted, the stones just stayed here.
EVIE: Right. Sitting around in the fields for a very long time.
JUSTIN: That saves a lot of walking. Not *all* the walking, but a lot.
EVIE: I love picturing it. A whole land of stones, waiting for someone to notice them.
JUSTIN: And then somebody did. I wonder who.

*Scene 2 — Who built them? (1:15)*
[They walk along the side of the biggest hunebed, Borger. Wide shot: a 23-metre-long monument.]
EVIE: And this is the largest one in the country. It's about twenty-three metres long. It has nine big capstones lying on top.
JUSTIN: Nine. Twenty-three metres. That's like… forty Justins nose to tail.
EVIE: Forty Justins would be a very loud parade.
JUSTIN: A very *fluffy* parade.
EVIE: People built it about five thousand years ago.
JUSTIN: Who?
EVIE: Early farmers. A group we call the Funnel Beaker people. They lived in villages, grew crops, and kept animals.
[Flashback: a small village with round huts, fields, people carrying baskets.]
JUSTIN: So they weren't giants. They were regular people.
EVIE: Regular people with big ideas.
JUSTIN: And they built a stone house?
EVIE: Not for living. A resting place. A grave for their community.
JUSTIN: Oh. So we're being extra quiet.
EVIE: Yes. And paws stay on the path.
JUSTIN: Paws on the path. Got it. I'm basically a very polite statue.
EVIE: When I stand here, I can almost hear them. Children laughing, baskets rustling, someone calling everyone to come help.
JUSTIN: And nobody had a phone to call with. Just a very loud voice.

*Scene 3 — The big experiment (1:45)*
[Inside the museum or on a grass patch: a small model with rollers and ropes. Animated.]
JUSTIN: Okay. But *how* did they move them? These stones can weigh a lot.
EVIE: Some of the heaviest weigh around twenty thousand kilograms.
JUSTIN: That's… roughly four elephants.
EVIE: Four elephants, sitting on one rock, and somebody had to move it.
JUSTIN: And the elephants weren't going to help.
EVIE: Nobody knows for certain how they did it. But scientists have ideas.
[Storybook animation, three ideas appearing like cards.]
EVIE: Idea one: logs. Put the stone on wooden rollers and push or pull it with ropes.
JUSTIN: Like skating, but heavier. But wait. Why do rollers help? Why not just pull?
EVIE: Because dragging means the whole rock rubs on the ground. Rollers turn, so the rock rolls along instead of rubbing.
JUSTIN: Like a skateboard under a box. Fewer scrapes.
EVIE: Fewer scrapes, and much less pulling.
EVIE: Idea two: sledges. A stone on a wooden sledge, dragged across the ground.
JUSTIN: Add a dozen strong friends. Or fifty.
EVIE: Like pulling a sled across snow, except the sled is carrying a mountain's cousin.
JUSTIN: A mountain's cousin. I'm stealing that.
EVIE: Idea three: slopes of earth. Build a ramp, slide the stone up, put it in place.
JUSTIN: Wait, a ramp made of *sand*?
EVIE: Then take the ramp away again. The stones stay.
JUSTIN: Honestly, that's smart. You build the thing that helps you build the thing.
EVIE: And some experts say we can't be sure even the rollers were really used. We still have questions.
JUSTIN: I like that. A mystery with a few good guesses.
EVIE: Sometimes the best part of history is the part we're still wondering about.
JUSTIN: Wondering is my favourite hobby. Right after napping and snacks.

*Scene 4 — Teamwork (1:00)*
[Montage: a hundred storybook villagers pulling a rope together; mothers with babies watching; children carrying water.]
EVIE: But here's the part I love. No one person could do this alone.
JUSTIN: So it took a whole village.
EVIE: Dozens and dozens of people, pulling together.
JUSTIN: Pulling together in one direction. That's quite clever. If everybody pulls a different way, the stone goes nowhere.
EVIE: Except maybe in a very confused circle.
JUSTIN: A confused stone. Poor thing.
EVIE: Not only clever. *Kind.* It's like they were saying: we are together, and this place will remember us.
JUSTIN: So the stones aren't just heavy. They're full of everyone who helped.
EVIE: That's what I dream about when I look at them. All those hands on one rope.
[Paw tags glow softly.]

**CONCEPT EXPLAINED (2:00)**
[Cutaway: a long timeline drawn like a ribbon with little icons: ice, stones, farmers, castle, windmill, cats.]
EVIE: Justin, do you want to know the secret of this place?
JUSTIN: Always.
EVIE: It's not only about stones. It's about *time.*
JUSTIN: Time? But I can't hold time. I can't even hold a sock.
EVIE: Then let's draw it. Imagine time is one long ribbon, and we can only stand at the very end.
[Cards appear, one at a time, each with a paw-stamp.]
EVIE: One — **Long, long ago.** Ice carried the stones, tens of thousands of years before anyone built anything.
JUSTIN: Two — **A very long time ago.** About five thousand years ago, farmers used them to build.
EVIE: Three — **Today.** We can still walk beside them.
JUSTIN: Hold on. So the ice is way back there, the farmers are somewhere in the middle, and we're at the end?
EVIE: Yes. And the ribbon between each card is longer than you'd think.
JUSTIN: Five thousand years. That's eighty lifetimes, one after another.
EVIE: Imagine eighty grandmas in a row, each handing the story to the next.
JUSTIN: And my grandma still forgets where she put her glasses.
EVIE: And the stones are still here. Still sleeping.
JUSTIN: So history isn't far away. It's lying right here in the grass.
EVIE: Like a very old friend who never moved house.
JUSTIN: A friend who never says anything, but you can tell it's listening.
EVIE: And we keep it safe by being careful with it.
JUSTIN: Careful paws, careful hearts.
[Gentle music swell.]

**RECAP + TEASER (0:45)**
JUSTIN: Recap! How do you move a giant stone?
EVIE: With ice, long ago, to bring them here…
JUSTIN: …and with teamwork, logs, ropes and maybe ramps, to build with them.
EVIE: And a lot of people who cared about each other.
JUSTIN: And a lot of time, because history is a ribbon, not a dot.
EVIE: Look at you, Professor Justin.
JUSTIN: Thank you. I'll be sending my invoice in snacks.
EVIE: Deal.
JUSTIN: Next time, I want a place that's the *opposite* of heavy stones. Something light. Something… windy.
EVIE: Ooh, a desert?
JUSTIN: In the Netherlands? (grinning) Maybe.

**GOODNIGHT OUTRO + CLIFFHANGER (0:30)**
[Dusk. Fog rolls across the heath beyond the trees. Music slows. The kittens sit together near the path, looking at the sleeping stones.]
EVIE (yawning): They look so peaceful.
JUSTIN: Goodnight, giants. Thanks for waiting five thousand years for us.
[Paw tags glow. Then— *ting… ting…* A clear bell, close now. The cats freeze.]
EVIE (whisper): Justin. That's the same sound.
JUSTIN (whisper): From the Island… and the Village…
[Wide: the heath at dusk. Two small shadows against the fog, one short and fluffy, one bouncy. Both with little bells.]
JUSTIN (whisper): Evie. There are *two* of them.
[Cut to black. Title card: "Next: The Dutch Desert".]

## Shot notes / visual ideas
- Stones should feel huge: low angle, cats tiny at their feet. Keep the cats on the path, never on the stones.
- Heath at dusk in the last shot: purple heather, thin fog, silhouettes only. Do NOT reveal the lambs' colours yet (no faces; just two bell glints).
- Timeline ribbon: simple icons, big paw-stamps, minimal text.
- Flashbacks: flat storybook; ice = blue-white, villagers = earthy colours.
- Bell motif: first heard faintly in Ep.2 and Ep.3, clear here. Keep the same *ting* sound for Cotton's bell in Ep.5 (see lead/CHARACTERS.md).
- Low-compute friendly: stills + parallax + mouth swap.
